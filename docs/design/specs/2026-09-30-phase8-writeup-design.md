# Phase 8 Design — The Write-up

Written for: whoever implements Phase 8.

**Status:** approved in design review 2026-09-30; awaiting spec review.
**Depends on:** Phases 0–4, 6 and 6b, all merged to `main` (PRs #1–#6).
**Produces:** a portfolio-facing README and report, two headline figures, blueprint status
corrections, and a test that keeps every cited number honest.

---

## 1. Purpose and audience

The audience is **hiring managers and technical interviewers.** The write-up has two layers:

- **`README.md`, the front door.** A reader should get the result in about 2 minutes.
- **`docs/REPORT.md`.** A technical interviewer should be able to read it in about 15 minutes.
  The per-phase documents are linked from it for depth.

**The result leads, honestly.** Within-project prediction works. Cross-project transfer does
not. The report then says what Phases 6 and 6b learned about why. This is the blueprint's own
fallback framing (§3), which applies because Scenario B fell below the baseline once the
label-replay features were removed. **The rigour comes second:** pre-registered validity gates,
a pre-registered hypothesis test, and negative results reported rather than hidden. For this
audience that is the project's differentiator.

**Success criterion:** a reader cannot come away believing a claim the committed artifacts do
not support.

## 4. README (the front door)

In this order:
1. **One-sentence problem.** Predict, at PR-open time, whether a pull request will wait more than
   7 days for its first human review.
2. **The result in three lines.** Scenario A P@10 against the baseline, with its interval;
   Scenario B falling below the baseline without the repo's own history; and one sentence on what
   Phases 6 and 6b found.
3. **Figure 1.**
4. **Why it's trustworthy.** Three bullets: leakage-proof chronological replay; pre-registered
   validity gates at every phase; negative results reported.
5. **Reproduce it.** The pipeline commands in order. State plainly what reproduces from the
   committed state (the figures and the claims test) and what needs a GitHub token and a
   multi-hour re-collection (everything upstream of `data/features/`).
6. **Repo map and links** to `docs/REPORT.md`, the per-phase documents and the blueprint.

## 5. Report outline (`docs/REPORT.md`)

1. **Problem, and why ML beats a rule.** Normal wait times vary hugely by repo, author and
   backlog. State the prior-art angle from blueprint §2 in one paragraph.
2. **Data.** 45 repos collected and 39 kept; the two-stage, two-tier collection; the label
   (D5, with D3 as a sensitivity check) and the sensitivity table from Phase 2; the
   `anthropics/skills` pilot as a case study.
3. **Leakage prevention.** Chronological replay; the "resolvable by t" rule for label-derived
   features; fidelity flags kept as columns, never as features; the brute-force audit.
4. **Evaluation design.** Scenario A (time cutoff) and Scenario B (leave-repos-out, 5-fold
   `GroupKFold`); P@10 per repo and AUC-PR; the trailing-90-day baseline; the cluster bootstrap
   over repos.
5. **Results.** Scenario A's headline; Scenario B; the three ablations; and the **pool-size
   caveat**, meaning B's raw P@10 exceeds A's only because B's per-repo candidate pools are
   ~7× larger, so AUC-PR is the fair A-vs-B comparison.
6. **Why doesn't it transfer?**
   - **Phase 6:** the two FULL models lean on the same features in near-identical proportions
     (label-replay share 60.9% A, 63.1% B). Differential reliance does not explain it.
   - **Phase 6b:** the pre-registered repo-fingerprinting test landed in **PARTIAL_SHAP_ONLY**.
     State T and I with their intervals, the reliance shares, and the two caveats in §9 verbatim.
     Say that the hypothesis was formed post-hoc and then tested under a rule fixed in advance.
7. **Fairness.** The newcomer-vs-repeat paired gap in both scenarios, stated as the null (and
   marginal null) it is.
8. **What didn't work, and limitations.**
   - Phase 5 (survival) was not built, so **blueprint §4's Scenario B bar (C-index > 0.65) was
     never measured.** Say so plainly.
   - 39 repos is a small sample for any cross-repo claim; B's per-fold AUC-PR swings from 0.507
     to 0.965.
   - Snapshot features: 2026 repo statistics applied to 2024 PRs.
   - Star strata are defined by 2026 star counts.
   - `experiments.csv` logs all 36 runs. Summarise what was tried and did not help.
   - One-line lessons from the incidents: a mutation check that overwrote a gitignored
     artifact (verify by hash, not `git diff`); tests that asserted nothing (mutation-check them).
9. **How it was built.** The practices, in project voice: pre-registered gates that test
   validity rather than success; a pre-registered hypothesis test with its decision rule
   committed before any code; mutation-checked tests; independent review of every change; and
   every figure and number regenerable from committed artifacts.
10. **Reproducing it.** The same commands as the README, with expected runtimes where known.

## 6. The two headline figures (`writeup_figures.py`)

Both are rendered from **committed** artifacts only. Nothing is retrained, and nothing under the
gitignored `data/models/`, `data/predictions/` or `data/features/` is read.

- **Figure 1, `figures/headline_transfer.png`.** AUC-PR for Scenario A and Scenario B, each
  showing FULL, NO_LABEL_REPLAY and the trailing-rate baseline. Source: `data/phase4_runs.json`.
  - **Phase 4 recorded no AUC-PR intervals, so the figure draws none.** Scenario A is a single
    point per bar. For Scenario B, each bar is the mean of its five folds, with the five
    per-fold values overlaid as dots. The dots show the real spread; no uncertainty is invented.
  - The title states the finding in words.
- **Figure 2, `figures/fingerprint_scatter.png`.** Two panels, seen repos (A) and held-out repos
  (B). Each point is one repo: its mean repo-feature SHAP contribution `c` against its actual slow
  rate `y`, with ρ annotated in each panel. Source: `data/phase6b_transfer.csv`.

The script writes deterministic output. Colours and fonts may be chosen freely; values may not.

## 7. The claims test

`writeup_claims.py` holds a registry. Each claim has:
- a **name**;
- a **source function** that recomputes the value from a committed artifact;
- the **exact string** the value must render as (a format applied to the recomputed value);
- the **documents** it must appear in (`README.md`, `docs/REPORT.md`, or both).

`tests/test_writeup_claims.py` asserts two things for every claim. The recomputed value, once
formatted, equals the registered string; this catches a stale artifact or a formatting change.
And that string appears in every listed document; this catches a mistyped or missing number.

**Source hierarchy, in order:**
1. **A committed data file, recomputed.** For example, the A/FULL `precision_at_10` in
   `data/phase4_runs.json`, the label-replay share summed from `data/phase6_importance_*.csv`,
   ρ_A and ρ_B recomputed with Spearman from `data/phase6b_transfer.csv`, and the cohort count
   and D5 slow rate from `data/phase2_gate.json`.
2. **Otherwise, the committed generated phase document, parsed.** Some values exist only in
   rendered text: 6b's bootstrap intervals, Phase 6's fairness paired differences, and Phase 4's
   pool-size medians. The parse must match the value's labelled context, not just any number.
3. **Never a gitignored artifact.** A reader who clones the repo cannot check those.

**Scope:** about 25 claims, covering every headline number, every interval, and the numbers
inside the §9 caveats.

**Falsifiability, in both directions:** editing a cited number in a document must fail the test,
and so must editing the artifact it comes from. The implementation plan names a mutation for
each direction.

## 9. Honesty constraints

These bind the prose. Each is also covered by the claims test wherever it carries a number.

1. **The unmeasured bar.** Blueprint §4's Scenario B target (C-index > 0.65) was never
   measured, because Phase 5 was not built.
2. **Pool size.** B's raw P@10 (0.796) exceeding A's (0.769) is a pool-size artifact. Use
   AUC-PR to compare the two scenarios.
3. **A post-hoc hypothesis.** 6b's hypothesis came from an exploratory gain-importance peek, and
   was then tested under a decision rule committed before any code.
4. **The 6b caveats, carried from the final review.** Use these, or equivalent wording that keeps
   every number:
   - "The observed ρ_B = +0.32 is not the ≈0 the spec predicted, but its descriptive bootstrap
     interval (about [0.00, 0.57]) makes it only marginally distinguishable from it." That
     interval is recomputed from `data/phase6b_transfer.csv` with a seeded paired bootstrap over
     repos. It is a **descriptive** statistic, outside the pre-registered verdict, and must be
     labelled as such.
   - "Per-fold intervention deltas on B range from +0.245 to −0.131, and I's 95% interval
     [−0.043, +0.106] contains zero; the data cannot distinguish a small intervention effect
     from none."
   - **Do not cite P(I ≤ 0) = 0.22.** It needs row-level predictions, which live in the
     gitignored `data/predictions/`, so it fails §7's source hierarchy.
5. **Fairness.** Scenario A's newcomer gap is a null. Scenario B's is a marginal null: its paired
   interval's lower bound (−0.010) sits close to zero, while the newcomer slice on its own is
   over-predicted. State all three facts.
6. **Sample size.** 39 repos is small for any cross-repo claim.
7. **No upgrade of 6b.** PARTIAL_SHAP_ONLY must never be restated as "supported" or
   "confirmed", in any section.

## 10. Testing

- **The claims test** (§7). Every claim passes, and the plan's mutation checks, one per direction,
  are caught.
- **A figure smoke test.** `writeup_figures.py` writes both PNGs into a `tmp_path` directory from
  committed data, and never into `figures/` during tests. The PNGs are non-empty and the function
  is deterministic.
- **The existing 240 tests** still pass.

## 11. Out of scope

Phase 5 (survival), Phase 7 (the Streamlit demo), any retraining or new modelling, any new
statistic beyond the one descriptive ρ_B interval in §9, and any change to a phase's generated
document or its generator.
